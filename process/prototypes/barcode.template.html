Ten phone calls, one cell per utterance

Each strip is one call, read left to right from start to finish. Each cell is one utterance, colored by the kind of unit it turned out to be. The blue that stands out most from the background marks complete sentences spoken with no filler and no repair.

    How to read it

    Point at, tap, or tab to a strip and use the arrow keys to read any utterance. In the pop-up, a dotted underline marks fillers, editing terms, and words the speaker went on to repair. The number at the right is the share of the call made of fluent complete sentences.

    What the colors mean

    Blue cells are complete clauses: the stronger blue when fluent, the quieter blue when they contain a disfluency. Orange is a clause the speaker left unfinished. Green is a fragment with no clause. Grey is a response token such as "yeah" or "uh-huh", which is not a failure of any kind.

    Where the data comes from

    Ten of the 650 parsed Switchboard conversations, chosen to span the range from few fluent complete sentences (top) to many (bottom), counted by utterance. They are a hand-picked spread, so the shares here are not corpus averages. Laughter and other units with no words are left out.
